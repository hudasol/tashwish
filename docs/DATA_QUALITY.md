## Definitions used in this report

- **Gulf aircraft:** unique ICAO24 with at least one trace point in the box that day (includes ground vehicles).
- **Airborne (metric population):** not a non-transponder emitter (`adsb_icao_nt`), not on the ground.
- **Quality present:** share of airborne rows with a NIC value, after carrying the most recent aircraft object forward at most 120 s.
- **Own quality:** share of airborne rows whose own trace point carried the aircraft object (the rest are carried forward).
- **NIC<4 / NACp<5 / NACp<8:** share of airborne rows with quality, per-row (not per-aircraft). These are descriptive row shares, not the detection statistic.

## Findings that shaped the design (each is reproducible from the tables above and the code)

1. **Ground vehicles dominate the raw point count.** Rows from `adsb_icao_nt` (non-transponder emitters such as airport vehicles per the readsb documentation) are all on the ground and are excluded from aircraft metrics. Their share is in the `vehicle_rows_excluded` column against `airborne_rows`.
2. **Quality is carried by a minority of points.** `own_quality` is well below `quality_present`; the carry-forward rule (120 s) recovers the rest. Over all ingested days, the gap between consecutive own-quality rows of an aircraft has median 16.6 s, 95th percentile 79.2 s and 99th percentile 8560.8 s.
3. **Coverage is thin.** The number of airborne Gulf aircraft per day (column `airborne_aircraft`) is a few hundred, and `min_hourly_aircraft` shows how few are visible in the quietest hour. This is a property of the volunteer receiver network, not of air traffic itself, and is why the coverage model gates every claim.
4. **Duplicate (icao24, ts) keys** occur almost only for ground vehicles: 24229 of 24264 duplicate keys over all ingested days are `adsb_icao_nt` on the ground. They are excluded with the vehicles.
