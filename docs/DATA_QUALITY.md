## Definitions used in this report

- **Gulf aircraft:** unique ICAO24 with at least one trace point in the box that day (includes ground vehicles).
- **Airborne (metric population):** not a non transponder emitter (`adsb_icao_nt`), not on the ground.
- **Quality present:** share of airborne rows with a NIC value, after carrying the most recent aircraft object forward at most 120 s.
- **Own quality:** share of airborne rows whose own trace point carried the aircraft object (the rest are carried forward).
- **NIC<4 / NACp<5 / NACp<8:** share of airborne rows with quality, per row (not per aircraft). These are descriptive row shares, not the detection statistic.

## Findings that shaped the design (each is reproducible from the tables above and the code)

1. **Ground vehicle rows are a large part of the raw point count.** Rows from `adsb_icao_nt` (non transponder emitters such as airport vehicles per the readsb documentation) are all on the ground and are excluded from aircraft metrics. Their count is in the `vehicle_rows_excluded` column against `airborne_rows`; they outnumber the airborne rows on 8 of 21 days.
2. **Quality is carried by a minority of points.** `own_quality` is well below `quality_present`; the carry forward rule (120 s) recovers the rest. Over all development days, the gap between consecutive own quality rows of an aircraft has median 19.7 s, 95th percentile 78.7 s and 99th percentile 10706.4 s.
3. **Coverage is thin.** The number of airborne Gulf aircraft per day (column `airborne_aircraft`) ranges from 444 to 2034, and `min_hourly_aircraft` (lowest 22) shows how few are visible in the quietest hour. This is a property of the volunteer receiver network, not of air traffic itself, and is why the coverage model gates every claim.
4. **Duplicate (icao24, ts) keys** occur almost only for ground vehicles: 295744 of 296907 duplicate keys over all development days are `adsb_icao_nt` on the ground. They are excluded with the vehicles.
